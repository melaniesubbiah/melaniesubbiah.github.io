I am a human-centered AI researcher focused on mutual interpretability: models that can interpret implicit meaning in human communication, and whose behavior humans can interpret in turn.

I am currently a postdoc in Computer Science, working with Ellie Pavlick at Brown University. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Columbia University, advised by Kathleen McKeown. Prior to graduate school, I worked on GPT-3 at OpenAI (co-first-author on Language Models are Few-Shot Learners), and machine learning research at Apple.

I am broadly interested in how models interpret and reason about implicit aspects of people and situations, and ways to make this process transparent and controllable for the people interacting with these systems. My current work is on: (1) improving long-term memory in chatbots across multi-session interactions, (2) preserving human well-being, agency, and ownership in AI collaborations, and (3) building systems of collective AI governance that protect people’s data and guide AI development. I primarily work with language models but am curious to explore interpretive challenges in other modalities as well.
