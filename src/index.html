I am a human-centered NLP researcher focused on mutual interpretability: language models that can interpret implicit meaning in human communication, and systems whose behavior humans can interpret in turn.

I am currently a postdoc in Computer Science, working with Ellie Pavlick at Brown University. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Columbia University, advised by Kathleen McKeown. My thesis focused on interpretation and interpretability in subjective domains. Prior to graduate school, I worked on GPT-3 at OpenAI (co-first-author on Language Models are Few-Shot Learners), and machine learning research at Apple.

My work is unified by a central focus on prioritizing human well-being in how we develop and use AI, including respect for human expertise, lived experience, and data privacy. I am currently interested in: (1) improving long-term memory for chatbots across multi-session interactions through continual learning in weights, in-context effects and external memory, (2) protecting users by ensuring safe responses in sensitive conversations and preserving human agency, control, and ownership in AI collaborations, and (3) building systems of collective AI governance that protect people’s data and provide avenues to challenge and direct AI development. My goal is humanistic AI grounded in mutual interpretability.
